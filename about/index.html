Personal Information

I am a postdoc at The University of British Columbia in Vancouver, Canada. My research focuses on environmental transport processes, including river turbulence, granular flows, and fluid-driven sediment transport. I am educated in multiple disciplines, with PhD training in geomorphology and hydrology, and MSc training in statistical and condensed matter physics. This interdisciplinary background has enabled me to produce unique research that blends ideas from physics and geoscience.

Research Interests

I am currently studying fluid-driven sediment transport in river channels with a mixture of laboratory experiments, analytical models, and computational simulations. My work leans heavily on physics and computer vision techniques. Since turbulent flows and granular dynamics are inherently stochastic, a portion of my work is also devoted to the fundamental study of stochastic processes with applications in geoscience.

  1. Analytical modeling: I describe the movements of individual sediment grains using ideas from kinetic theory and the physics of active matter.

  2. Experimental studies: I conduct experiments on sediment transport processes in laboratory channels with emphasis on fluid turbulence and grain-scale processes.

  3. Computational physics: I produce discrete element simulations to represent sediment transport, integrate partial differential equations to describe flows, and construct Monte Carlo algorithms to simulate stochastic processes.

  4. Stochastic processes: I investigate dynamical systems driven by complex noise sources, such as non-Gaussian noises, random on-off switches, and intermittent sequences of pulses.
